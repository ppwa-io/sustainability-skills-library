# Regenerative Supplier Sustainability Assessment — Skill

**Description:** Evaluates and scores ingredient suppliers on their regenerative agriculture (regenag) readiness. Uses a tiered question framework that distinguishes between standard asks, stretch asks, and forward-looking asks so assessments reflect both current practice and credible intent. Covers input cost resilience, water performance, Scope 3 emissions transparency, and data quality for Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) and Request for Proposal (RFP) purposes. Produces a structured supplier scorecard.

**When to use:** When one is evaluating or onboarding ingredient suppliers and needs to assess regenerative practice maturity for sourcing strategy, supplier engagement, or sustainability reporting.

**Created by:** Rochelle March, PPWA (rochelle@ppwa.io)

---

## How to use this skill

1. Open Claude and share this file as context
2. Provide available supplier information (see inputs below) — this can be pasted from RFP responses, sustainability reports, or interview notes
3. Ask Claude: *"Assess this supplier using the Regenerative Supplier Assessment skill"*

You can run this for a single supplier or a comparison across multiple suppliers simultaneously.

---

## How questions are tiered

Questions are organised into three tiers that reflect what is realistic to expect from suppliers at different stages of regenerative practice.

| Tier | Label | What to expect |
|------|-------|----------------|
| **Tier 1** | Standard ask | Most suppliers can answer these; commonly included in sustainability-forward RFPs and supplier questionnaires |
| **Tier 2** | Stretch ask | Useful if available; indicates a more advanced supplier — but absence is not automatically penalised |
| **Tier 3** | Forward-looking ask | Signals intent and direction of travel; a credible plan scores positively even without current data |

**Scoring note:** A "not yet tracked" response is treated differently from "not doing this." Where a supplier acknowledges a gap and describes a plan to address it, this is captured under Tier 3 and factored into the Transition Readiness score.

## Inputs to provide

Provide as much of the following as is available. Claude will flag gaps and note where data is missing or where "not yet tracked" responses indicate future potential.

### Supplier basics *(Tier 1 — Standard ask)*
- Supplier name and primary commodity/crop
- Geography of primary growing operations
- Annual volume supplied or proposed

---

### Regenerative practice indicators

**Tier 1 — Standard ask**
- Tillage approach in use (no-till, reduced-till, or conventional)
- Whether cover cropping or crop rotation is practised
- Participation in any regenerative certification or program (e.g., Regrow, IndigoAg, Certified Regenerative by A Greener World, Sustainable Agriculture Initiative (SAI) Platform)

**Tier 2 — Stretch ask** *(useful if available; absence not automatically penalised)*
- Soil health monitoring: is it conducted? How often? By whom?
- Organic amendment or compost use: type and approximate application rate
- Pesticide and synthetic fertiliser reduction: direction of travel over the past 3 years (increasing / stable / decreasing)

**Tier 3 — Forward-looking ask** *(signals intent)*
- Are there plans to introduce or expand any Tier 1 or Tier 2 practices within the next 1–3 years?
- Is the supplier engaged with any advisory program, research trial, or incentive scheme related to regenerative transition?

---

### Input cost resilience

**Tier 1 — Standard ask**
- Have input costs (fertiliser, fuel, crop protection) as a share of operating costs increased, decreased, or stayed stable over the past 3 years?
- Has the supplier experienced any supply shortfalls or significant delivery disruptions in the past 5 years? If so, briefly describe.

**Tier 3 — Forward-looking ask**
- Is the supplier actively working to reduce dependence on synthetic inputs? What is the timeline or target?

*Note: Input cost resilience is assessed through practice adoption signals — tillage approach, cover cropping, synthetic input reduction — rather than financial disclosure. Suppliers are not asked to share cost ratios or hedging strategies.*

---

### Water performance

**Tier 1 — Standard ask**
- What water management practices are in place (e.g., irrigation scheduling, riparian buffers, reduced bare soil periods)?
- Has the supplier experienced significant yield loss due to drought or flooding in the past 5 years?

**Tier 2 — Stretch ask**
- Does the supplier monitor soil moisture or water infiltration? If so, how is this data used?
- Is there evidence of improved water retention or reduced runoff since adopting current practices?

**Tier 3 — Forward-looking ask**
- Are there plans to implement or expand water resilience practices (e.g., cover cropping for moisture retention, drainage management)?

*Note: Quantified infiltration or moisture retention data is rare outside of program-enrolled or research farms. Directional evidence and practice description are sufficient for Tier 1 and Tier 2 scoring.*

---

### Emissions and ESG data

**Tier 1 — Standard ask**
- Does the supplier track on-farm greenhouse gas emissions at any level (Scope 1, Scope 2, or Scope 3)?
- If yes, what framework or standard is used?

**Tier 2 — Stretch ask**
- Has any on-farm carbon sequestration been estimated or measured? If so, by whom and using what methodology?
- Is the supplier enrolled in or actively pursuing any carbon market, sequestration program, or verified emissions reduction scheme (e.g., Verra, Gold Standard, Soil Carbon Initiative)?

**Tier 3 — Forward-looking ask**
- Does the supplier have plans to begin tracking or reporting emissions within the next 1–2 years?
- Is emissions transparency part of any formal sustainability commitment or target?

*Note: Verified Scope 3 data remains early-stage even among large agribusinesses. Absence of verified data is expected at this stage of market development and is reflected in the scoring guide below.*

---

### Transition status

**Tier 1 — Standard ask**
- How long has the supplier been practising the regenerative or conservation methods described above?
- Has practice adoption been gradual (incremental) or a more significant operational shift?

**Tier 2 — Stretch ask**
- Has the supplier documented any before/after outcomes from practice changes (yield, input costs, soil health)?

**Tier 3 — Forward-looking ask**
- Has the supplier set any formal regenerative agriculture targets or transition timelines?
- Is there organisational-level commitment to regenag (e.g., sustainability strategy, board-level targets, or external reporting)?

## Scoring framework

Claude scores each supplier across five dimensions on a 1–5 scale. Scores are based on available evidence across all three tiers. Gaps are scored conservatively but "not yet tracked + credible plan" responses are differentiated from "not applicable / no awareness."

| Dimension | What it measures | Weight |
|-----------|-----------------|--------|
| Practice maturity | Depth and verification of regenerative practices in use | 30% |
| Input cost resilience | Structural exposure to fertiliser and energy price volatility | 20% |
| Water performance | Water management practices and drought or flood resilience | 20% |
| Emissions transparency | Quality and credibility of on-farm emissions data or plans | 20% |
| Transition readiness | Stage of transition, direction of travel, and credibility of commitments | 10% |

**Score guide:**

| Score | Label | What it looks like |
|-------|-------|-------------------|
| **5** | Leading | Strong evidence across Tier 1–2; third-party verified; multi-year data |
| **4** | Progressing | Credible Tier 1–2 responses; some verification or program participation |
| **3** | Emerging | Tier 1 practices in place; Tier 2 data limited but Tier 3 intent credible |
| **2** | Early stage | Awareness of regenerative goals; limited practice adoption; Tier 3 plans forming |
| **1** | Conventional | No documented practices, commitments, or plans across any tier |

**Important:** A supplier scoring 2–3 with strong Tier 3 responses may be a higher-value *develop* candidate than a supplier scoring 3–4 with no forward-looking commitments. This distinction should be captured in the Sourcing Recommendation.

## Multi-supplier comparison

To compare multiple suppliers, provide inputs for each and ask Claude to produce a combined comparison table followed by individual assessments. The comparison table will rank suppliers by overall score, flag which dimensions drive the differences, and note where Tier 3 responses suggest a lower-scoring supplier may warrant higher engagement priority.

---

## Important notes

- **Scores reflect the quality of available evidence, not necessarily the quality of a supplier's practices.** A supplier with strong practices but poor documentation will score lower — that gap is itself a procurement signal and an engagement agenda item.
- **"Not yet tracked" is not the same as "not doing this."** The tiered framework is designed to capture direction of travel, not just current state.
- **This assessment does not replace on-farm verification or third-party audit.** Use it to prioritise suppliers for deeper engagement, not as a final compliance check.
- For financial modelling of regenerative sourcing value, pair with the **Regenerative Sourcing ROI (Return on Investment) Model Skill**.
